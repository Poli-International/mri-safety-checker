MRI Safety Checker — Body Jewelry

Look up what a body jewellery material is made of and what that means around a scanner, so you can tell the radiographer the right thing. This is a materials reference, not a clearance.

Read this first. ASTM F2503 (Standard Practice for Marking Medical Devices and Other Items for Safety in the Magnetic Resonance Environment) is how a manufacturer marks an item it has actually tested. Those marks belong to a specific tested product, not to a material in general, and nothing here has been tested or marked under it. What follows is our own plain-language summary of what each material is and how materials like it tend to behave near a scanner. It is not a clearance and it cannot tell you whether your jewellery can stay in. Only the radiographer and the MRI safety staff at your scan site can decide that. Tell them what your jewellery is made of and who made it, and take this page with you if it helps the conversation.

Accepts material names, alloy grades, and common aliases. Covers 17 body jewelry materials including polymers, implant-grade metals, and ferromagnetic alloys.

Clinical disclaimer: This tool is for informational purposes only and does not replace clinical judgement or site-specific MRI safety protocols. Always confirm jewelry material and MR condition with the attending radiographer or MRI safety officer before scanning. Patients with implanted devices should follow their implant-specific guidance. MR Conditional classifications depend on field strength, SAR limits, and scan duration — confirm these parameters with your facility.
