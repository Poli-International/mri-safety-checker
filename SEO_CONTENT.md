## What Is MRI Safety Checker, Body Jewelry?

The MRI Safety Checker, Body Jewelry is a free web tool that classifies 17 common body jewelry materials under the ASTM F2503 standard. Users can search by material name or browse all materials grouped by their MRI safety classification: MR Safe, MR Conditional, MR Unsafe, or Verify Grade.

The tool provides detailed information for each material, including field strength limits, SAR limits, clinical notes, common uses, and alternative names. It uses a dataset curated by Poli International that covers polymers (BioFlex®, PTFE, nylon), implant-grade metals (titanium, niobium, steel, gold, platinum), and unsafe materials (ferromagnetic steel, silver, magnetic jewelry).

The tool features a search interface with autocomplete, a browse view organized by safety classification, and expandable result cards that reveal full material specifications. All classifications follow ASTM F2503 (Standard Practice for Marking Medical Devices and Other Items for Safety in the Magnetic Resonance Environment).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 1. What is ASTM F2503?
ASTM F2503 is the Standard Practice for Marking Medical Devices and Other Items for Safety in the Magnetic Resonance Environment. It defines three classifications: MR Safe, MR Conditional, and MR Unsafe. The MRI Safety Checker tool uses this standard to classify body jewelry materials.

### 2. Is titanium jewelry safe for MRI?
Implant-grade titanium (ASTM F136) is classified as MR Conditional up to 3T with SAR limits of 4 W/kg. Most radiographers accept titanium jewelry, but some facilities require removal for head or neck scans. Always confirm with the attending radiographer.

### 3. Can I wear BioFlex jewelry during an MRI?
Yes. BioFlex is classified as MR Safe with no known hazards in any MR environment. It is non-ferromagnetic, non-conductive, and generates no RF heating or MRI artifact. It is the gold standard for retainer jewelry during MRI procedures.

### 4. Why is sterling silver listed as MR Unsafe?
Silver is diamagnetic with very low susceptibility, but it can generate RF heating and significant susceptibility artifact in MRI images. Most MRI facilities require removal as a precaution. It is not certified for MRI use.

### 5. What does "MR Conditional" mean?
MR Conditional means the material has no known hazards under specified conditions. These conditions typically include maximum field strength (e.g., 3T) and SAR limits (e.g., 4 W/kg). The jewelry is safe only when these conditions are met.

### 6. How many materials does the tool cover?
The tool covers 17 body jewelry materials, including polymers (BioFlex, PTFE, nylon), implant-grade metals (titanium, niobium, steel, gold, platinum), and unsafe materials (ferromagnetic steel, carbon steel, silver, nickel silver, magnetic jewelry).

### 7. Can I use this tool for implanted medical devices?
No. This tool is specifically for body jewelry materials. Patients with implanted medical devices should follow their implant-specific guidance and consult their healthcare provider.

### 8. What if my material isn't in the dataset?
If a material is not found in the dataset, treat it as unverified and consult your radiographer before scanning. Consider switching to a BioFlex retainer for guaranteed MR Safe status.

### 9. Does the tool replace clinical judgement?
No. The tool is for informational purposes only and does not replace clinical judgement or site-specific MRI safety protocols. Always confirm jewelry material and MR condition with the attending radiographer or MRI safety officer before scanning.

### 10. What are the field strength limits for implant-grade steel?
Implant-grade steel (ASTM F138) is classified as MR Conditional typically up to 1.5T with SAR limits of 2 W/kg. However, many MRI sites require removal due to potential heating and image distortion in sensitive scan areas.
